FAQ

Land Intelligence Questions

Which of my listings is this for?

Land, new homesites, and homes on acreage, where the ground is part of what the buyer is paying for. For an in-town home or a town lot, a full package is usually more than the buyer needs; the 3D fly-thru or the listing graphics are a better fit there.

Why fly in winter?

With the leaves down, a mapping flight sees through bare hardwoods to the ground: trails, old roads, fence lines, wet spots, and terrain that are hidden under summer canopy. Cold nights are also when a thermal flight can point to where a home may be losing heat through its roof and walls, which is why the closing gift is a first-winter check.

Why combine the flights with public data?

Public data covers what a camera can’t. LiDAR measures the bare ground and the height of the trees, even under heavy cover, and the USGS, NRCS, FEMA, and Wisconsin DNR layers add history, soils, floodplain, and wetlands. But public data is a few years old and has no current photo. The flights are current and far more detailed. Together they give a current, high-detail picture of the property on top of a reliable public record, and the drone and LiDAR check each other.

Is the home thermal check a home inspection?

No. It points to likely heat loss on the night it’s flown, as indications for a qualified contractor or energy auditor to verify. It’s a separate service from the listing flights, flown on a cold, calm night, so its date depends on the weather. It doesn’t replace a home inspection. On a listing, the house is only included if the seller agrees, and the findings are shared only if the seller chooses.

Was the thermal survey flown on this property?

Yes. The thermal map and wildlife count on the Country Property page come from a Matrice 4T flight of this property on the evening of November 24, 2026. The aerial was flown with the Matrice 4E the same morning. The property is vacant wooded land, so there is no house in the example.

Will the maps work in an outdoor app?

Yes. Every layer comes in one Google Earth file (KMZ) that opens on a phone with a single tap. Terrain features, cover, edges, and contours also come as GPX and KML files for outdoor apps such as onX, Gaia GPS, HuntStand, and BaseMap, along with a printed map and a live web map.

Who pays for New Owner Stewardship?

The first home thermal check is the agent’s closing gift, bought at an agent rate. After that, the owner books and pays for any ongoing checks or surveys directly with Novak Geospatial Solutions™. The New Owner Stewardship page says so plainly.

Does this fit EXIT’s brand standards?

The pages use the EXIT logo and colors, with your office’s name, address, and the listing agent on every page. If your franchise brand standards call for changes, they’re made once in the template and carry to every listing after that.

Is this a land survey?

No. The maps are produced from drone imagery and public data for planning and visualization. They are not a land survey and do not establish property boundaries.
