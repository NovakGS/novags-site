Why Test Drone Mapping Accuracy at All?

When a contractor bills from a stockpile volume or an engineer designs from an elevation, “does it look right?” isn’t enough. The question is “is it right, and by how much?”

The industry already has a yardstick for that: the ASPRS Positional Accuracy Standards, used by engineers, surveyors, and public agencies.

Nothing requires a drone company to follow it. That is exactly why doing it says something.

Why Publish the Whole Process?

Accuracy claims are easy to make and hard to check. So Novak Geospatial Solutions™ publishes the whole test: the method, every checkpoint, what went wrong, and what’s still open. Anyone deciding whether to rely on drone data can see the work for themselves on the accuracy page.

It’s also how the Novak|GS tagline, Elevated Data | Grounded Truth, gets earned rather than just claimed. The data comes from the air. The proof comes from the ground, and it’s published for anyone to check.

How the Test Worked

Ground Control Points (GCPs) – The Anchors
5 surveyed targets that pin the map in place.
Checkpoints – The Grading Key
33 surveyed targets the software never sees, used to grade the map.
  1. 01Lay Targets39 across an active gravel pit.
  2. 02Survey ThemGPS rover, plumb on each center.
  3. 03Fly175 ft up, 8,317 photos.
  4. 04Build the MapUsing the 5 anchors only.
  5. 05Grade ItAgainst all 33 checkpoints.

A check on the check: seven targets were surveyed a second time, hours later. The two readings agreed within 0.04 ft, well inside what the standard allows.

What Went Wrong (And What Was Done About It)

No test goes perfectly. The standard asks that every problem be documented, and that no point be thrown out just because it looked bad.

  • 01Targets Marked on Their EdgesSix automatic marks were about a foot off center.Re-marked by hand.
  • 02Map Sat 0.2 ft HighOne processing setting trusted the drone’s GPS too much.Set to trust the ground targets.
  • 03Wind Folded a TargetCheckpoint CP01 blew over during the flight.Excluded, with photo proof.
  • 04Re-Shots Didn’t SaveOnly eight afternoon re-checks were kept.Graded on the seven good pairs.
  • 05Survey Mark Read LowHeight checks on a federal survey mark came in 0.04 to 0.16 ft low.Vertical claim held until resolved.
  • 06Map Leaned NorthwestAbout 0.06 ft on average, under an inch.Investigated and reported, not corrected away.

Common Questions About Drone Mapping Accuracy

How Accurate Is Drone Mapping?

In this test, 0.10 ft (about 1.2 in) horizontally and 0.09 ft (about 1.0 in) vertically on open, bare ground. Vegetation, steep slopes, and water make it harder.

What Is the Difference Between a Ground Control Point and a Checkpoint?

Ground control points anchor the map. Checkpoints are kept hidden from the software and used to grade the finished map.

Do Clients Get This Check on Every Job?

Every measured job gets checkpoints, and the client gets the accuracy results with the data. The full ASPRS test is repeated once a year and whenever the main equipment changes.
