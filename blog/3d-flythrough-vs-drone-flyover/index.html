A flyover shows the house. A 3D fly-through shows the property.

Ground photos sell the inside of a home. On acreage, buyers also want the outside: how the home sits on the land, where the ponds and trails are, and what the property actually includes. A drone flyover gets part of the way there. A fly-through built from a measured 3D model gets the rest.

A flyover is one pass through the air

A standard drone flyover is live footage. The pilot flies a path, and the video is whatever the camera caught on that path: limited by wind, light, trees, wires and the altitude the flight allows. Want a slower look at the house, or a different angle on the back pond? That means another flight.

On a small lot, that's fine. On 15 acres with ponds, trails and outbuildings, one pass rarely shows how it all fits together.

A fly-through moves through a measured model

A 3D fly-through starts with a mapping flight: more than 1,000 overlapping photos, each tagged with an RTK-corrected position, with the model tied to ground control points. Those photos are processed into a true-to-scale 3D model of the whole property.

The video is then flown through that model. The camera can go anywhere: low over the trails, slow past the home, high enough to show the whole parcel in one frame. Distances and layout are real, because the model is built from measurements, not stitched from footage.

Where it earns its place

The fly-through is built for properties where the land is part of the sale: acreage and hobby farms, waterfront, homes with outbuildings, trails or views, and parcels made up of more than one lot. Those are the listings where buyers have the most questions a ground photo can't answer.
