One Finished Picture, Not a Dozen Downloads

Every source above is public. Anyone can look up a flood map. Few buyers have the time, software, or training to pull all of it together for one parcel, because each piece comes from a different place and in a different shape:

  • Different websites: federal, state, and county agencies each publish their own pieces, on their own sites, with their own search tools.
  • Different formats: laser point clouds and elevation grids, GIS map files, scanned historical maps, and PDF records.
  • Different coordinates and heights: each source arrives in its own map projection and height reference, and they have to be lined up before one layer can be read against another.
  • Different languages: soil ratings, flood zone codes, and wetland classes take some training to read correctly for one property.

That combining is the product. I find each source, put everything in the same coordinate system, clip it to the parcel, line it up with the flights, and read it for that ground. The buyer opens one map and one report instead of a dozen downloads and an afternoon of guesswork, and you hand them an answer instead of a homework assignment.

Why Drone Mapping and Public LiDAR Work Better Together

LiDAR and drone mapping answer different questions. Each covers the other’s weak spot, and putting them on the same vertical datum lets one check the other.

Public LiDAR Airborne laser, statewide

  • Laser pulses reach the ground through gaps in the canopy, so the bare-earth model holds up under heavy timber.
  • Measures tree height directly, which is how canopy and cover classes were built here.
  • Free, state-wide, and consistent from one property to the next.
  • A few years old. It doesn't show this year's cuts, plots, or blowdowns.
  • No photo, and a coarser ground grid (2 ft here).

Drone Mapping Matrice 4E & 4T, Nov 2026

  • Current to the day of the flight.
  • A sharp aerial photo, far more detailed than satellite imagery.
  • Adds thermal from the same visit with the 4T.
  • A camera can't see through dense canopy, so the ground model is estimated under thick cover.
  • In leaf-off hardwoods, bare branches don't reconstruct, so the drone surface can't measure tree height.

Together

The public data supplies the ground, the trees, the water, the soils, and the records. The flights supply today’s picture and the thermal. On the 33-acre property shown in the package pages, the leaf-off drone surface sat almost on the ground under the bare hardwoods. That is ideal for seeing the forest floor, and exactly why the LiDAR is used for canopy height. Before any analysis, both datasets were put in the same coordinate system and heights (NAVD88, US survey feet) and compared on open, flat ground.

FAQ

Land Intelligence Questions

Why fly hunting land in winter?

With the leaves down, a mapping flight sees through bare hardwoods to the ground: trails, old logging roads, fence lines, wet spots, and terrain that are hidden under summer canopy. Conifers stand out against the bare timber, and winter flights happen after the season, when there is nothing to disturb. The maps are in hand for spring work on food plots, trails, and stand sites.

Why combine the flights with public data?

Public data covers what a camera can’t. LiDAR measures the bare ground and the height of the trees, even under heavy cover, and the USGS, NRCS, FEMA, and Wisconsin DNR layers add history, soils, floodplain, and wetlands. But public data is a few years old and has no current photo. The flights are current and far more detailed. Together they give a current, high-detail picture of the property on top of a reliable public record, and the drone and LiDAR check each other.

Can a thermal flight tell me where the deer are right now?

No. A thermal survey is a snapshot of one flight, delivered after processing. It gives a general picture of wildlife presence and where animals were bedded or feeding at that time. It is informational only and is not a real-time locating service. Animals move, and deer under thick conifer cover or out of the flight area are not counted.

Was the thermal survey flown on this property?

Yes. The thermal map and deer count on the Hunting & Recreation page come from a Matrice 4T flight of this property on the evening of November 24, 2026. The aerial was flown with the Matrice 4E the same morning.

Can one flight do it all?

A Matrice 4T mapping flight can capture elevations, contours, and thermal scouting in one mission. This example used the Matrice 4E for the most detailed map and the 4T for thermal.

Will the maps work in a hunting app?

Yes. Every layer comes in one Google Earth file (KMZ) that opens on a phone with a single tap. Terrain features, cover, edges, and contours also come as GPX and KML files for hunting apps such as onX, HuntStand, BaseMap, and Gaia GPS, along with a printed map and a live web map.

Who pays for Stewardship?

The buyer, after closing. Ongoing deer herd surveys and repeat flights are booked and paid for by the buyer, directly with Novak Geospatial Solutions™. The Stewardship page says so plainly.

Is this a land survey?

No. The maps are produced from drone imagery and public data for planning and visualization. They are not a land survey and do not establish property boundaries.
