Drainage Tile Locating by Drone

Old tile maps get lost, and some fields never had one. After a rain, the soil over a working tile line dries differently than the soil beside it. A drone flight with thermal and visible cameras can pick up those lines across a whole field and put them on a map.

How It Works

Timed to the Weather

  1. Watch the ForecastThe field is flown a day or more after a soaking rain, on bare or lightly covered soil.
  2. Fly the FieldThermal and visible images are captured over the whole field in one visit.
  3. Trace the LinesTile line indications are traced from the imagery and placed on a georeferenced map.
  4. Confirm on the GroundThe map goes on a phone, and lines are confirmed with a probe before any digging.

Plain Talk

Conditions Decide the Result

Tile locating from the air depends on soil moisture, ground cover, and timing. Some lines will not show on a given flight. If conditions aren't right, the flight is rescheduled instead of flown for a poor result. Always call Diggers Hotline (811) before digging.

FAQ

Drainage Tile Questions

How Does a Drone Find Buried Drain Tile?

Soil directly over a working tile line drains and dries faster than the soil beside it. For a short time after a rain, that difference shows as lines in thermal and visible-light imagery taken from above.

When Is the Best Time to Fly?

On bare or lightly covered soil, in spring before planting or in fall after harvest, a day or more after a soaking rain. The window is short, so flights are scheduled around the weather.

Will Every Tile Line Show Up?

No. Deep lines, plugged lines, heavy crop residue, and poor timing can all hide tile. The map shows the lines that appeared on the day of the flight and marks them as indications to confirm with a probe.

What Format Is the Map?

A georeferenced image and line file that open in Google Earth and most farm and GIS software, so the lines can be followed on a phone in the field.
