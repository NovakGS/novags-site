Before You Call

What to Have Ready

  • Search on foot first. A reasonable ground search comes before a drone search.
  • If you bump your deer, back away. Put a waypoint on your map at the last known location, and do not approach again for at least 4 hours, or longer if the shot calls for it (see below).
  • Know why the deer is likely dead. Shot placement, blood, and time since the shot all matter.
  • Note what the blood looks like. Pink, frothy blood with bubbles points to a lung hit. Bright red blood can mean the heart or an artery, dark red usually means liver, and green or brown matter with a stomach smell means a gut shot. Hair color at the hit site helps too. Use it to judge how long to wait (see below).
  • Get landowner permission for every property the search may cross.
  • Leave weapons behind. No firearms, bows, or crossbows during the search or recovery.
  • Mark the hit site and the last blood, and stay off the trail if possible.
  • Note the time of the shot and the deer's direction of travel.

How Long to Wait Before Tracking

Give the deer time to lie down and die before anyone follows the trail. Pushing a wounded deer too soon is one of the most common ways a recoverable deer is lost.

  • Double Lung30 minutes to 1 hour
  • Single Lung4 to 6 hours, or overnight
  • Liver4 to 6 hours
  • Gut8 to 12 hours, usually overnight

If you are not sure where the deer was hit, wait for the longer time. These are common hunter guidelines, not guarantees.

Wisconsin DNR Rules

Recovery Only, Never Hunting

Hunting with a drone is illegal in Wisconsin. A drone may help recover a deer believed to be dead; it may not be used to pursue or track a live animal. If a deer located during a search is still alive, the search ends, the drone is recalled, and no location information is shared. Hunters remain responsible for all Wisconsin hunting regulations, including tagging and registration.

Source: Wisconsin DNR, Drone Use Policy. This page is a summary, not legal advice.

FAQ

Deer Recovery Questions

Is Drone Deer Recovery Legal in Wisconsin?

Hunting with a drone is illegal in Wisconsin. Wisconsin DNR policy treats recovering a dead animal differently from hunting, and wardens weigh the circumstances of each flight. Novak|GS flies recoveries as an independent third party, only when there is good reason to believe the deer is dead, and with no weapons carried during the search. See the Wisconsin DNR drone use policy for details.

What Happens if the Deer Is Still Alive?

The search ends and the drone is recalled. No location information on a live animal is shared, because using a drone to pursue or track a live deer can be treated as hunting. The search fee and the located fee both apply, since the search was completed and the deer was located.

When Does a Thermal Search Work Best?

Evenings, early mornings, and cold, overcast days. Bright sun warms logs, rocks, and bare ground, which can mask a deer's heat. A deer cools toward the air temperature after it dies, so the sooner the search, the better.

  • 30 to 50°FBest range for thermal contrast
  • Under 50°FTypically detectable 24 to 48 hours after the shot
  • About 60°F and UpRoughly 4 to 12 hours after the shot
  • Under 30°FStill workable, but each flight covers a smaller area

Below 30°F, cold and wind shorten battery life and flight time, so the area a search can cover shrinks. Whether a cold-weather search is accepted depends on the time since the shot, how confident the hunter is in where the deer went, the size of that area, and conditions such as wind, snow, and freezing fog.

These are ranges reported by recovery operators, not guarantees. Sun, rain, snow, standing water, and heavy cover all change them.

Is a Recovery Guaranteed?

No. Thick cover, standing water, and time since the shot all affect what a thermal camera can find. A search covers ground much faster than walking it, but it does not guarantee a recovery.
