Soil ratings: Homes w/o basements and Septic fields are USDA NRCS ratings of how well the soil suits a house without a basement and a septic drain field. Not limited: the soil suits the use; expect good performance and very low upkeep. Somewhat limited: the soil has drawbacks that special planning, design, or installation can overcome; expect fair performance and moderate upkeep. Very limited: at least one soil feature, such as steep slope, wetness, or shallow bedrock, works against the use and generally takes major soil work, special design, or costly installation to overcome; expect poor performance and high upkeep. Not rated: NRCS has no rating for that soil and use. A Very limited rating doesn’t rule a site out, but it means planning on engineering and added cost.

What a Pond Here Would Involve

Every low spot inside the boundary with 2 to 60 acres draining to it was tested twice over the 2024 LiDAR bare earth, once with an 8 ft dam and once with a 12 ft dam. The three best sites are below, ranked by how much water each foot of dam holds back, and kept at least 300 ft apart. The map shows the pond an 8 ft dam would hold.

Pond screening is approximate. A pond needs a site survey, soil borings, an engineered design, and permits (county, and WI DNR where water or wetlands are involved).

How to read this table: Each site was tested with two dam heights, so each pond has two sets of numbers. Land draining to it is the ground that sheds rain and snowmelt toward the dam site. It is the pond’s water supply, not its size. Pond size is the water surface when full. Water held is in acre-feet; 1 acre-foot is about 326,000 gallons, or 1 acre of water 1 ft deep. Dam length is how far the earthen dam would run across the low ground. Pond 2 has the most land draining to it but holds the least water because its draw is steep: the ground rises about 8 ft in the first 100 ft behind the dam, so the water backs up only a short way. Pond 1 sits in a flatter draw, so an 8 ft dam backs water up nearly 300 ft. The soil rating uses the classes defined under the build-area table, rated for holding water in a pond.
