Why it works for acreage

What ground photos can't show

How the home sits on the landWhere the house is relative to the road, the ponds, the woods and the neighbors, in one view instead of pieced together from a dozen ground shots.
What's actually includedParcel-overlay versions draw the property lines on the photo, so buyers can see where the land starts and stops before asking.
Fewer wasted showingsBuyers who book have already walked the property from above and know it fits. The tire-kickers sort themselves out online.
A listing that stands outMost acreage listings still lead with a few straight-down stills. A 3D fly-through gets watched on the listing page, social and YouTube.

What the listing receives

Ready for MLS, social and the agent's website

3D fly-through video4K video from a measurable 3D model of the property, plus a 59-second cut sized for Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn.
Listing aerialsEdited high and low obliques of the home and grounds, delivered full resolution and web-sized for MLS upload.
Parcel-overlay photosMatching versions of the aerials with the property boundary drawn on, from county parcel records.
Optional: orthomosaicA current, high-detail map of the whole property from the same flights, sharper and newer than satellite imagery.

Boundary lines on parcel-overlay photos are drawn from county parcel data for illustration. They are not a land survey and do not establish property lines. All flights are conducted by an FAA Part 107 certified pilot.
