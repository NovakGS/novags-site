At Enchanted Valley Acres, just north of Cross Plains, Wisconsin, where the Christmas Trees season follows Fall Fun, we used the same mapping flights behind the property's site map to find and measure the trees in 30 blocks across 37 acres. Nobody walked a row with a tally counter. The count came from flights we were already making.The result is an inventory by retail size class, block by block, built for planning the season's sales.

Bold rows are the core retail sizes. Colors match the dots on the map. Heights come from the drone surface model, which tends to read conifer tips low, so class counts are estimates until the field check is in.

The farm's salable trees are concentrated in a handful of blocks. Five of the 30, S11, S7, S4, M5, and M6, hold about two-thirds of this season's 6 to 8 foot trees. The blocks near the visitor area tell a different story: scattered large trees with open ground between them, the pattern of blocks that have been cut through.

The biggest group on the farm is the 7,664 trees at 2 to 4 feet, led by S3, W5, and M4. That is the inventory that carries the next several seasons, and the count a grower needs to plan sales, staffing, and wholesale commitments.

Young seedlings, especially in the east field, are too small to count reliably from this flight, so they are undercounted here. Their rows show plainly in the photo, and a lower flight or the full-resolution imagery can count them.

The deliverable

One sheet the whole crew can read

The inventory prints as an 11×17 map sheet in the same format as the rest of the site package: the farm map on top, headline counts, and a table of every size class with its typical use, how many trees there are, and which blocks hold the most.

It answers the questions that come up every fall: how many trees can we sell this year, where do we send customers looking for an 8-footer, and how many seedlings do we order.

Every sheet states how it was checked: RTK fix rate, field-check results, and what was not verified.

How it works

Measure the canopy, then find the peaks

  1. 01Two surfacesThe mapping flight produces a model of the treetops and a model of the bare ground beneath them.
  2. 02Canopy heightSubtracting one from the other leaves the height of everything standing on the ground, every 4 inches.
  3. 03Find each topA conifer has a single peak, so each high point is a tree. Small candidates also have to show a conifer's dark green crown in the photo.
  4. 04One crown, one treeBig trees can show more than one peak. Crown size sets how close two trees can be, so a big tree is counted once, not twice.
  5. 05Check it in the fieldSample trees are measured on the ground and the match is reported with the inventory. For this farm, that check is next.
