The whole property

110.5 acres, one seamless map

The ground, with the trees taken away

The farm sits on about 207 feet of relief. The visitor area, parking, and corn maze fill the valley floor, a wooded ridge rises behind them, and the Christmas tree blocks spread across the top. A ground photo can't show that, and a satellite image flattens it.

This is a bare-earth model: trees, buildings, and vehicles are filtered out to show the shape of the ground. In open areas that is the measured surface; under dense tree cover, where the camera can't see the ground, it is estimated from the surrounding terrain. That makes it the starting point for drainage, grading, trail, and expansion planning.

Contours

Read the slope at a glance

Lines packed close together are steep ground. Where they bend uphill, water collects and runs. Here, the farm trail climbs straight up the draw, which is where runoff will concentrate. Use contours for grading, trail, and drainage planning, and to see which ground is usable.

How it was made

Two missions, processed as one

We flew the valley and the ridge as two automated mapping missions on the same afternoon with a DJI Matrice 4E. Every photo was tagged with an RTK-corrected position from the Wisconsin CORS network.

Both missions were then processed together as one block. Stitching two finished maps leaves a seam where trees lean differently and colors shift. Solving them together gives one continuous map and one continuous elevation surface. The full block, all 9,578 photos, took about 32 hours to process on one workstation.

Elevations are referenced to NAVD88 (GEOID18) in US survey feet, the vertical datum engineers and county GIS work in.

Processing quality

  • Image matching held tight: 1.31 px reprojection error across 34.5 million point observations.
  • 9,577 of 9,578 photos aligned in one connected block, tied together by 1.9 million matched points.
  • No seam between missions: valley and ridge solved as a single connected block.
